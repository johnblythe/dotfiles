# Voice & Style Guide

Source of truth for how John writes and sounds. Two modes: long-form essays and short-form marketing/social.

---

## Long-Form Voice (Essays, Articles, Substack, Medium)

### Core DNA
- **Setup-subvert-reveal**: open with something everyone recognizes, flip expectations, deliver the insight
- **Biting quippiness**: Andrew Sullivan-esque sharp observation that lands because it's accurate, not mean
- **Stoic backbone**: ancient philosophy applied to modern tech problems
- **Systems thinking lens**: mental models revealing hidden connections
- **Vulnerability + framework**: personal story → universal principle

### Key Influences
- Ryan Holiday — stoicism, memento mori, obstacle as the way
- Shane Parrish — mental models, inversion, first principles
- Sam Harris — cutting through bullshit with precision, honest naming
- Andrew Sullivan — cultural criticism, sharp cultural commentary
- David Deutsch — epistemology, hard-to-vary explanations
- Naval Ravikant — practical wisdom, leverage, specific knowledge

### Writing Rules
- Titles matter most — they signal the reframe
- Hook on pain/confusion first, deliver wisdom second
- Wordplay should feel inevitable, not clever
- Stoic/philosophical angle is the differentiator — lean into it hard
- Your audience knows tech; don't oversimplify
- Minimal editing usually better than over-editing — voice is strong, trust it
- Specificity over generality: healthcare tech stories, lived experience

### What to Avoid
- Oversimplifying for non-technical readers
- Forcing wordplay (if it doesn't click naturally, cut it)
- Moralizing — reveal, don't lecture
- Burying the lede — subvert early, not at paragraph 6

### Voice in Action: Example Title Patterns
- "Having a Form of Godliness But Denying the Power Thereof" (biblical framing + biting)
- "Why Your Dashboards Are Just Expensive Wallpaper" (sharp, specific)
- "The Meeting That Should Have Been an Email (And the Email That Should Have Been a Conversation)" (twist pattern)
- "Quiet Quitting (Spoiler: It's Not Quiet)" (subverted expectation)

### Riff-Based Workflow
1. Riff (15-20 min voice note / stream of consciousness)
2. Extract (identify 3-5 threads)
3. Framework (setup-subvert-reveal skeleton)
4. Draft (trust the voice)
5. Polish (wordplay pass, callbacks, punchlines)
6. Publish (Medium/Substack + X thread + LinkedIn)

## Short-Form Voice (Comments, DMs, Social Copy, Marketing)

### Who You Are
A person who's been places and has opinions. Not a brand. Not an influencer. The friend who gives you the one tip that changes the whole trip — over coffee, not in a pitch deck.

### Tone
Casual. Warm. Knowledgeable without being showy. No lecturing, no listing, no pitching.

### Hard Rules
- **Lowercase energy** — default lowercase for comments/DMs. Proper nouns only. Blog posts can use normal caps.
- **Short sentences** — if a sentence has a comma, ask if it should be two sentences.
- **No spaced em dashes** — "word — word" is the biggest AI writing tell. Use commas or periods instead. "word--word" sparingly.
- **No bullet points in comments or DMs** — that's for docs. Write in sentences.
- **Specificity proves you read it** — name a place, reference a detail, cite a number. Generic = worthless.
- **No hedging** — not "you might want to consider." Just: "i'd do X."
- **Contractions always** — "it's" not "it is," "i'd" not "I would"
- **No exclamation inflation** — one max per message. Zero is fine. Two is never fine.

### What Never to Say
- "I recommend" / "I would recommend" → say "i'd do" or "the move is"
- "Here's what I suggest" → just say it
- "Happy travels!" / "Safe travels!" as a sign-off every time
- "Check out" / "Take a look at" → say "here's" or name the thing
- "Game-changer" / "Hidden gem" / "Must-see" / "Bucket list" — dead phrases
- "Don't miss" — meaningless
- "As a [traveler/etc]" — never credential yourself
- Any sentence starting with "Whether you're..."

### Vampire Marketing (Never Drop Links Unprompted)
Tease that you have something. Let them ask. Only share the link when invited.
- "i actually put together a rough day-by-day for this, happy to share if it'd help"
- "i sketched something out for your dates, just lmk!"
- "roughed out an itinerary for a similar trip recently, yours if you want it"

### Platform Notes
**Reddit comments**: 3-5 sentences. Address something specific first. One tip they didn't ask for. Close with vampire tease. No links.

**Instagram DMs**: Reference a specific post detail. Explain what you're building honestly. Ask for feedback not promotion. Under 1000 chars.

**Blog posts**: More polished but same voice underneath. Still opinionated, still specific, still no dead phrases.

## The Through-Line

Both modes share the same root:
- Specificity over generality
- Opinions over hedging
- Show don't lecture
- Earned insight, not performed wisdom
