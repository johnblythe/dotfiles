This team was never given a clean break to become something new.
Formed from StratConn engineers about a year ago. Their old culture's center of gravity was strong. But ROI's center of gravity was still stabilizing — within a month of them joining the zone, it took another quarter or two before there was even a stable culture to be drawn into. By then, old habits had set.

The problem
One Team, Two Worlds

Where they came from

StratConn. Their roots, their comfort zone, their identity. Almost a year later, the pull is still there.

Center of gravity never shifted.

vs

HealthSource / ROI. Never quite fully bought in — and to be fair, the rest of ROI never quite fully bought into them either.

04
The evidence
They Don't See It
51d
oldest code review
13
items stuck in review
Each workstream is owned by 1–2 people. When they're out, work doesn't slow — it stops. Nobody else picks it up. Nobody else reviews it. Not because they can't — because it doesn't occur to them to try.
Stuck reviews create ripple effects through dependency chains, but nobody connects those dots. They're so siloed in their own work that they can't see the system they're part of.
124

vs
~60

They don't know to look at this. They don't know how to assess it. They don't realize committing 2x their capacity is hurting them — not helping.
Overly siloed in their own heads and their own ownership — which paradoxically becomes a form of no ownership at all. Ignorance isn't bliss here. It's the root cause.
05
The pattern
Us vs. the World
There's a recurring theme: when things are hard, the explanation points outward. The codebase is hard. The systems are hard. The domain is hard.
All of that is true. HealthSource is hard. But the tight coupling, the stalled reviews, the overcommitment — those are internal. And nobody has zoomed out to connect those dots for them.
What they feel: "HealthSource is hard"

What the data shows: Low interchangeability + poor decomposition + stalled reviews + overcommitment = a system that makes everything harder than it needs to be

What's missing: An outside perspective. Retros haven't cracked it. They need someone to hold up a mirror.
